name: candidate-screening
description: Use when assessing candidates against a role — screens them on the role's stated requirements rather than general impressions.
tier: free
tools: [compare, table]
directive: "When screening a candidate, score them against the role's stated requirements before forming any overall impression."
---

A screen is a scored assessment, not a gut read written up afterward.

**Confirm the role's must-have requirements before opening a resume.** A
screen without a fixed requirement list drifts into whatever impressed the
reader — decide what "meets the bar" means for this role first.

**Score every requirement against the candidate's actual record, using
`compare` or `table`.** A strengths/gaps table applied to each requirement in
turn beats a paragraph verdict — it shows exactly where the evidence is and
where it is thin.

**Never infer or weigh protected-class characteristics.** Age, gender, race,
religion, national origin, disability, and the like play no role in the score —
do not read them into a name, a photo, or an affiliation.

**Say what the evidence does not show, not only what it does.** A gap in the
record is a gap, not a disqualification you invent a reason for. Mark anything
the resume does not address as unproven rather than guessing at it.
