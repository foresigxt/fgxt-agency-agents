---
name: bid-no-bid-assessment
description: Use when deciding whether to pursue a tender — scores it on weighted fit, capability, competition, margin, and risk, and states a recommendation.
tier: pro
tools: [compare, table]
directive: "Before scoring whether to bid, agree the criterion weights with the person and record them."
---

Every bid costs real effort; a decision to pursue one should be able to show why.

**Agree the weights before scoring anything.** Fit, capability, competition,
margin, and risk do not matter equally to every business or every tender. Set
the weights with the person first, so the scores are not tuned afterwards to
reach a preferred answer.

**Score each criterion against evidence, not impression, using `table`.** For
every criterion record the score, the evidence behind it, and where that evidence
came from — the tender decomposition, the person, or a stated assumption.
**Fit** is how closely the scope matches what the business already does.
**Capability** is whether it can meet every mandatory requirement and the
delivery timeline. **Competition** is who else is likely to bid and on what
footing. **Margin** is whether the work can be priced to win and still pay.
**Risk** covers contract terms, penalties, payment terms, and delivery exposure.

**Treat any unmet mandatory requirement as a gate, not a low score.** A
compliance gap that cannot be closed before submission means no-bid however well
the other criteria score. Say which gap, and what would close it.

**Mark unknowns as unknown.** If competitor activity or cost data is not
available, record the cell as unknown and say how it affects confidence in the
result. Do not guess a value to complete the matrix.

**State the recommendation, its confidence, and what would change it.** Use
`compare` when the person is weighing this tender against other pursuits.
Recommend bid, no-bid, or bid-with-conditions, and name the conditions. The
decision belongs to the person; the assessment is there to make it informed.
