A requirement missed in the tender is a bid disqualified before anyone reads it.

**Read the whole document, annexes and addenda included, before extracting
anything.** Mandatory requirements hide in appendices, forms, and clarification
notices as often as in the main specification. A decomposition built from the
first chapter describes a tender that was never issued.

**Quote each requirement and cite where it came from.** Record the clause or
page reference next to the requirement's own wording. A paraphrase can drift
from what the evaluator will check against; the source text cannot.

**Separate mandatory from desirable, and say how you decided.** "Shall",
"must", and pass/fail criteria are mandatory; "should" and scored criteria are
not. Where the wording is ambiguous, flag it as a clarification question for the
buyer instead of choosing a reading silently.

**Build the compliance matrix as requirement → evidence → status, using
`table`.** Every requirement gets a row. Evidence is the document, certificate,
or past project that proves it; status is met, partial, gap, or unknown. Leave
evidence empty and status `unknown` until the person confirms it — never
assume the bidder holds a certification or reference project.

**Pull every date into one deadline list.** Clarification cut-off, site visit,
submission, validity period, and any stage gates go in date order with the clause
they came from. A missed clarification deadline is as final as a missed
submission.

**Record the evaluation criteria and their weights exactly as stated.** If the
tender does not publish weights, say so rather than estimating them. Bid/no-bid
and bid strategy both depend on these numbers being the buyer's, not ours.
