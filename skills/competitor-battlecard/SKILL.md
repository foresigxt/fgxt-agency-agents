A battlecard earns its use in a live deal, not in a research doc nobody opens under pressure.

**Name the competitor and the decision it serves before gathering anything.** A
battlecard for a renewal conversation and one for a cold-deal objection lead with
different material. Fix which one you are building before searching.

**Lead with objections and rebuttals, not a feature list.** The reader is about
to be challenged, not educated. Put the top objections and their rebuttals first;
push capability rundowns and packaging detail to supporting proof points.

**Separate verified fact from inference, and label which is which.** A rival's
price, headcount, or roadmap is often reported secondhand. Mark anything you
could not verify as an assumption and say why it is plausible, using `compare` or
`table` so the label survives the format.

**Keep it defensible.** Cite the source behind every non-obvious claim. Stay
factual — a card built on disparagement instead of evidence loses the room the
moment it is challenged back.
