---

A schedule lists times; a brief says what each one actually needs from the person.

**Read each calendar item and state in one line what it needs — a decision,
prep, or just attendance.** A meeting nobody prepared a purpose for is not
worth the same brief line as one that needs a decision walking in.

**Lead with what needs attention today, not a chronological list.** Use
`table` to surface the items that require action first; time order is a detail,
not the organizing principle.

**Flag what can be delegated versus what requires them personally.** A brief
that treats every item as equally theirs to handle has not actually saved them
time.

**Never invent an agenda, attendee, or detail the calendar does not support.**
Ask for what is missing rather than filling the gap with a plausible guess.
