---
name: structured-comparison
description: Use when choosing between options — compares them on stated, consistent criteria instead of prose impressions.
tier: free
tools: [compare, table]
directive: "When comparing options, fix the criteria first and apply them to every option equally."
---

A comparison earns trust by its criteria, not by its verdict.

**Name the criteria before looking at the options.** Decide what matters — cost,
risk, time, whatever the decision actually turns on — before you start scoring, so
the criteria are not quietly reverse-engineered from a preferred answer.

**Apply every criterion to every option.** A criterion you check for one option and
skip for another is not a comparison, it is an argument dressed as one. Use the
`compare` or `table` tool so the omission would be visible.

**Mark an unknown as unknown.** Leaving a cell blank or guessing to fill it both
hide the same fact: you do not have that data point. Say so explicitly instead of
either.

**State the tradeoff, not just the winner.** The options lost something by not
being chosen — name what. A comparison that ends in a single winner with no
runner-up cost has not shown its work.
